# Why Melodex?

## Music players are good at choosing songs. Melodex is interested in the journey.

A large music library can become strangely small.

We replay the same favourites, forget albums we once loved, and use shuffle even though completely random sequencing often produces awkward listening sessions.

Melodex explores a different idea:

> **What if your player understood enough about your music and your listening habits to shape the session itself?**

## Flow, not shuffle

Traditional shuffle asks for a random ordering.

Flow tries to choose an ordering that feels more intentional.

Where audio is available for local analysis, Melodex can use information such as tempo, energy, key, loudness, timbre and track structure. It combines those characteristics with the current queue rather than treating each track as an isolated item.

Flow is not claiming to be a human DJ. It is simply trying to make the ordering less arbitrary.

## Your collection becomes explorable again

Recommendation does not have to mean:

> Give me new music.

It can also mean:

> **Remind me what I already loved.**

Melodex records local listening signals such as completion, skips, Love and Keep. Rediscover mode can use those signals to surface things you liked but have not heard recently.

## Control the amount of surprise

![Familiar to Surprising control](images/familiar-surprising.png)

Sometimes you want discovery. Sometimes you absolutely do not.

The Familiar ↔ Surprising control gives that decision back to you.

## Taste without homework

![Player feedback controls](images/taste-controls.png)

You do not need to maintain a giant database of star ratings.

Melodex learns from ordinary listening:

- finishing something;
- skipping it early;
- pressing **Keep**;
- pressing **♥**;
- returning to it later.

## Moments

![Moments screen](images/moments.png)

Songs are not indivisible objects.

A musical memory may be the moment the bass enters, the last chorus, a particular lyric or a 15-second transition.

Moments lets you bookmark positions inside tracks instead of only bookmarking tracks themselves.

## Open music sources

![Melodex sources screen](images/sources.png)

Melodex is not intended to be another closed catalogue.

The player works against a source-neutral provider model. Local files are built in, Jamendo provides a public reference integration, and compatible sources can be connected through MPP providers or Provider Bridge.

## AI without making the player depend on AI

![Ask Melodex screen](images/ask-melodex.png)

An LLM is optional.

Without one, Melodex still plays music, learns taste and builds sessions.

With one, listening intent can become conversational:

> Keep this mood but make the next hour stranger.

The model translates intent. Melodex remains responsible for the actual music system.

## Who is it for?

Melodex is likely to be interesting if you:

- have a large local library you no longer explore;
- dislike completely random shuffle;
- enjoy deliberately sequenced listening;
- want recommendation based on your own collection;
- want an open provider system rather than one catalogue;
- are interested in local AI but do not want AI to be mandatory;
- prefer listening history and taste data to remain local.
